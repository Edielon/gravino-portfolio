How we work

Two-Storey Residential Building: Structural Modelling and Preliminary Cost Study

01Project overview

A preliminary study for a two-storey residential building, prepared by Engr. Hanny Creselle Gravino. The steel frame alternative was modelled in ETABS, and the civil works for a steel frame and a reinforced concrete frame were estimated side by side to compare the two framing options early.

Building
Two-storey residential building
Scope
Structural modelling (steel frame) and preliminary cost study
Estimates
Reinforced concrete frame, and steel frame under two labour assumptions
Status
Preliminary study

02Structural model

  • Steel frame
  • Concrete pedestals and tie beams
  • Floor areas
Levels
Pedestal level 1.2 m above the base; second floor 3.4 m above it; roof deck a further 3.4 m
Grid
Lines A to G by 1 to 5 (with 3′), 20.0 m by 10.0 m
Steel frame
Wide-flange (H) columns and beams
Substructure
500 × 500 mm concrete pedestals and 300 × 400 mm footing tie beams
Floors
125 mm composite deck at the second floor; 125 mm slab at the roof deck

The model shown is the steel frame alternative. No ETABS model of the reinforced concrete frame is part of this study, and analysis and design results are not summarised here.

03Preliminary cost comparison

The same building, priced two ways. Each comparison uses a different labour assumption for the steel frame; they are alternatives, not separate projects or savings to be added together.

Comparison A · Labour based on 40% of material cost

Labour cost estimated at 40% of material cost.

Reinforced concrete frame, total direct cost

The steel frame alternative is estimated to cost ₱424,185.00 less than the reinforced concrete frame alternative under this labour assumption.

Comparison B · Steel-work labour based on ₱25/kg

Steel-work labour estimated at ₱25 per kilogram of steel.

Steel frame, total direct cost
₱2,765,386.96
Reinforced concrete frame, total direct cost
₱3,278,154.11

The steel frame alternative is estimated to cost ₱512,767.15 less than the reinforced concrete frame alternative under this labour assumption.

In this estimate, ₱25/kg applies to the 17,716.53 kg of steel works (steel and its bolts and connections); concrete and formwork labour for the steel frame stays at 40% of their material cost. The reinforced concrete frame is the same estimate as in Comparison A, with labour at 40% of material cost.

Preliminary estimates to compare framing options, not quotations. The differences are estimates, not achieved savings.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Don't see yours?

Ask anything, big or small. A short message is enough to start.

Where do you work?

Building and retrofit works are in the Philippines, starting in Mindanao. Project management, structural design and estimating can be delivered remotely to clients anywhere, including Australia.

Which services can you do remotely?

Project management, structural design and analysis, and cost estimating with BOQ and BOM. We work from your drawings and share models, schedules and reports online.

What should I send for a quote?

The location, the type and size of the project, your timeline and a budget range if you have one. Drawings or photos help, but they're not required to start.

How does a quote work?

We read your request, ask any questions and arrange a site visit if needed, then send a written scope and quote. There's no obligation to go ahead.

Can you work with my architect or contractor?

Yes. Coordinating architects, contractors and suppliers is part of how every project above was delivered.

My building was damaged in an earthquake. Where do I start?

Get in touch with photos of the damage. A damage assessment comes first, then a retrofit design and plan if it's needed.
